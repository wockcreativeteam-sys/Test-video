The model

1 · The strike

An impulse J gives the ball its launch speed. Hitting above or below centre (height h) also sets it spinning.

v₀ = J / m ω₀ = 5·J·h / (2·m·R²) I = ⅖·m·R² (solid sphere)

2 · Sliding

Right after the strike the ball skids. Kinetic friction slows it and spins it up until the contact point stops slipping.

u = v + ω × r_contact a = −μs·g · û du/dt = −(7/2)·μs·g · û

3 · Rolling

Once u = 0 it rolls without slipping. Only rolling resistance and air drag remain, so it slows gently.

v = ω·R a = −(μr·g + 5/7·k·v²) · v̂ k = ½·ρ·Cd·πR² / m

4 · Wall impact

The normal part of velocity reverses and loses energy. Friction at the wall trims the sideways part, and the wall absorbs some of the spin.

vn′ = −e·vn Δvt ≤ μw·(1+e)·|vn|, ≤ 2/7·|vt| energy lost = ½m·vn²·(1−e²)
  • Natural roll: set strike height to +0.8 (h = 0.4 R). The ball rolls from the first millisecond and never shows the sliding phase.
  • Stun and draw: set it to −1 and strike straight at a wall. The ball comes back off the wall faster than a centre hit, because its backspin is already turning it away.
  • Ice vs carpet: same strike, different floor. On ice the ball keeps sliding for a long time; on carpet it barely reaches the first wall.
